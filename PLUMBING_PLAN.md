# ADU Plumbing Plan: Analysis and Comparison with Architectural Standards

## What the Current plumbing.svg Shows

The plumbing plan is generated by `plumbing/gen_plumbing.py`, which calls
`render_floorplan_svg()` in plumbing mode. The drawing contains the following
layers:

### Water Supply Lines

- **Cold supply main** (blue, 1" stroke): A polyline running from the well
  connection east of the building, underground along the south and west
  exterior, then entering at the washer location and routing internally along
  interior walls (IW8, IW2, IW2o, IW2s) to the north kitchen wall (W9-W10),
  terminating at the ice maker position. Ten waypoints (bp1-bp10) define the
  route.

- **Cold WH stub** (blue): A short vertical T-stub branching north from the
  cold main to the water heater location.

- **Hot supply west** (red, 1" stroke, offset 1.5" from cold): Runs from the
  washer through the same interior wall path as the cold line, breaking at the
  water heater where it T-stubs north on both sides (west and east segments).

- **Hot supply east** (red): Continues east from the water heater break to the
  dishwasher center along the north wall.

- **Hot supply 2nd** (red, offset 3" from cold): A secondary hot line running
  from the north toilet east side through IW2/IW2o/IW2s to the kitchen sink,
  with T-connections back to the first hot line at both endpoints.

### Drain/Utility Path

- **Green utility path** (18" stroke width): A wide green band representing the
  buried utility trench running from the washer location, south along the west
  exterior wall, then east along the south side to the east property boundary.
  Fades via a gradient at the east end with a "from Well" label.

### Fixture Representation

- Plumbing-relevant fixtures are rendered at their layout positions: washer,
  toilets (2), utility sink, bath sink, fridge, kitchen sink, dishwasher, ice
  maker, water heater, and shower.
- Non-plumbing items are suppressed: dryer is hidden, furniture is entirely
  skipped (`_render_furniture` returns immediately), stove is excluded from
  kitchen appliances, and the hamper is omitted.
- The building interior (appliances, kitchen) is rendered at 60% opacity as a
  ghosted background to keep pipe routing visually prominent.
- Door/window openings are rendered at 20% opacity.

### Water Services Table

A tabular schedule rendered below the title block with columns: Fixture, Cold,
Hot, Drain. Lists 10 fixtures with checkmarks indicating which services each
requires. The water heater is excluded from the table (it is infrastructure, not
a fixture consuming water services). This table is defined both in the hardcoded
renderer (`_render_supplies_table`) and in the app data model
(`app/plumbing.py:FIXTURE_DEFS`).

### Property Boundary Lines

Two dashed brown lines show the south (216.73') and west (275.08') property
boundaries, clipped to the building vicinity. The SW property corner is marked
with a circle and label. Boundary line positions are computed by inverting the
site plan's building-to-PDF coordinate transform.

### Building Outline

The full building outline with inner wall faces is rendered normally. Dimension
lines are shown at 50% opacity, with the south E-W dimension shifted 9" to
clear the utility path.

### Title Block

Standard title block with "Plumbing Plan" title, inner area calculation, and
drawing metadata.


## Comparison with Traditional Architectural Plumbing Drawings

### Riser Diagrams

Traditional plumbing plans include **riser diagrams** showing vertical pipe
routing through floors, walls, and ceilings. These are essential for multi-story
buildings and show how supply and waste stacks connect vertically.

**Current state**: Not present. The ADU is a single-story slab-on-grade
structure, which reduces the need somewhat, but even single-story buildings
benefit from a riser diagram showing the vertical relationship between the
underground utility trench, slab penetrations, stub-ups at fixtures, and any
above-ceiling horizontal runs.

### Isometric Drawings

Professional plumbing submissions typically include a **3D isometric view** of
the piping system, drawn at 45-degree angles to show the complete DWV and supply
system in a single comprehensible view. Isometrics are the standard way to
communicate the full piping layout to both plumbers and inspectors.

**Current state**: Not present. The plan is strictly a 2D overhead view.

### Line Conventions

Standard practice uses distinct line styles to differentiate pipe types:

| Pipe Type        | Traditional Convention           |
|------------------|----------------------------------|
| Cold water       | Solid line, often blue           |
| Hot water        | Dashed or long-dash line, red    |
| Sanitary drain   | Thick solid or double line       |
| Vent             | Dashed or center-line pattern    |
| Gas              | Dot-dash line                    |

**Current state**: The drawing uses color coding (blue = cold, red = hot,
green = drain/utility) with uniform solid strokes. There are no dash patterns to
distinguish pipe types when printed in black and white. The drain path uses a
wide stroke (18") to represent the trench rather than a pipe centerline with
diameter annotation. There are no vent lines.

### Symbol Conventions

Standard plumbing symbols (per ANSI/ASME Y32.4 and typical architectural
practice) include:

- **Cleanouts** (CO): circle with a cross or "CO" label at accessible pipe
  junctions
- **Floor drains**: circle with radiating lines
- **Traps**: U-shaped symbol at each fixture drain connection
- **Shut-off valves**: bowtie or diamond symbol on supply lines
- **Check valves**: directional valve symbol
- **Pressure regulators**: diamond with "PRV"
- **Hose bibbs**: faucet symbol at exterior locations
- **Water meter**: circle or rectangle at service entry
- **Backflow preventers**: standard symbol at service connection
- **Tee/elbow fittings**: symbols at pipe junctions showing fitting type
- **Pipe direction arrows**: flow direction indicators

**Current state**: None of these symbols are present. Pipe junctions are simple
polyline bends (stroke-linejoin="round"). There are no valve, cleanout, trap, or
fitting symbols anywhere in the drawing. The "from Well" text label is the only
indication of supply source.

### Fixture Schedules

A traditional fixture schedule is a table listing each fixture with:
- Fixture type and manufacturer/model
- Required supply connections (hot/cold) with pipe sizes
- Drain connection size
- Trap size
- Fixture unit ratings (supply and drainage)
- Required air gap or backflow protection

**Current state**: The Water Services table lists fixtures with boolean
cold/hot/drain indicators. It does not include pipe sizes, fixture unit ratings,
trap requirements, or model specifications. The table is more of a connection
matrix than a proper fixture schedule.

### Pipe Sizing and Material Annotations

Professional plans annotate every pipe run with:
- Nominal pipe diameter (e.g., "3/4\" CW", "1/2\" HW", "3\" PVC")
- Material specification (PEX, copper, PVC, ABS, cast iron)
- Slope for drain lines (e.g., "1/4\"/ft" written along the pipe)
- Total length of runs for material takeoff

**Current state**: No pipe diameter callouts appear on the drawing. The
`drain_pipe` properties dict includes `"slope": "0.25 in/ft"` and
`"buried": True`, but these are data-model attributes not rendered on the SVG.
No pipe material is specified anywhere.

### Vent System (DWV)

A complete plumbing plan must show the vent system per the Uniform Plumbing Code
(UPC) or International Plumbing Code (IPC):
- Individual fixture vents or wet vents
- Vent stack and stack vent
- Air admittance valves (AAVs) if permitted
- Vent termination through roof

The vent system is critical for drain function (preventing siphonage of traps)
and is always required for permit.

**Current state**: No vent system is shown. The drain path is a single utility
trench with no individual fixture drain connections, trap details, or vent
routing.

### Code Compliance Annotations

Building departments expect to see:
- Minimum fixture clearances from walls
- Trap arm lengths (distance from trap weir to vent connection)
- Maximum distance to nearest vent
- Drain slope notation on each horizontal run
- Anti-scald valve notation at shower/tub
- Expansion tank at water heater
- T&P relief valve discharge routing
- Seismic strapping requirements (in seismic zones)
- Insulation requirements for hot water pipes

**Current state**: None of these annotations are present.

### Legend/Key

Traditional drawings include a legend defining all line types, symbols, and
abbreviations used.

**Current state**: No legend. The color coding (blue/red/green) is implicit.
The "from Well" label partially serves as a source annotation but is not part of
a formal legend.


## What's Missing vs. Traditional Drawings

Ranked by significance for permit review:

1. **Vent system**: No DWV vent routing is shown at all. This is the single
   largest gap; no plumbing permit will be issued without it.

2. **Isometric drawing**: The standard submission format for residential plumbing
   permits. Many jurisdictions require an isometric as the primary plumbing plan
   document rather than (or in addition to) a plan view.

3. **Pipe sizing**: No diameter callouts on any pipe run. The plan must show
   sizes for all supply and waste pipes based on fixture unit calculations.

4. **Individual fixture drain connections**: The green utility path shows only
   the main buried trench, not the individual drain branches from each fixture
   to the main line. Trap locations are not indicated.

5. **Plumbing symbols**: No cleanouts, valves, fittings, traps, or other
   standard symbols. At minimum, a main shut-off valve, cleanout access points,
   and fixture traps should be shown.

6. **Drain slope annotations**: The data model stores slope values but they are
   not rendered. Each horizontal drain run needs a slope callout.

7. **Material specifications**: No indication of whether supply is PEX, copper,
   or CPVC; no indication of drain material (PVC, ABS, cast iron).

8. **Fixture schedule**: The Water Services table needs pipe sizes, fixture unit
   ratings, and trap sizes to serve as a proper fixture schedule.

9. **Water heater details**: No T&P relief valve discharge path, no expansion
   tank, no seismic strapping notation.

10. **Hose bibb / exterior connections**: No exterior faucet locations are shown.

11. **Legend**: No formal legend defining line types and symbols.

12. **Gas line**: If any gas appliances are planned (water heater, stove), a gas
    line routing would be on the plumbing plan. Currently absent.


## Recommendations

### Essential for Permit Submission

These items would typically be required before a building department accepts a
plumbing plan for review:

1. **Add a DWV isometric drawing** — Create a separate isometric view showing
   all drain, waste, and vent piping in 3D schematic form. This is the single
   most important addition. For this ADU, the isometric would show the main
   building drain, individual fixture branches with traps, and the vent routing
   up through the roof. Consider adding a `plumbing/gen_isometric.py` generator.

2. **Add pipe size annotations** — Perform fixture unit calculation per UPC/IPC
   Table 6-4 (drainage) and Table A-1 (supply), then annotate every pipe segment
   with its nominal diameter. For this ADU: likely 3/4" cold main, 1/2" branches
   to fixtures, 3" building drain, 2" shower/toilet drains, 1-1/2" sink/washer
   drains, 1-1/2" to 2" vents.

3. **Show individual fixture drain connections** — Draw drain branches from each
   fixture to the main drain line. Currently only the main utility trench is
   shown. Each fixture needs a trap and a branch line to the main.

4. **Add vent routing** — Show vent pipes from each fixture (or wet-vent
   groups) to a vent stack through the roof. Can be on the plan view and/or
   the isometric.

5. **Add standard plumbing symbols** — At minimum: main shut-off valve at
   building entry, cleanout at each direction change in the drain, P-traps at
   each fixture, hose bibb locations, water heater T&P discharge.

6. **Add a legend** — Define all line types (solid blue = cold supply, solid red
   = hot supply, solid green = sanitary drain, dashed = vent) and all symbols
   used.

7. **Add drain slope callouts** — Annotate each horizontal drain run with its
   slope (typically 1/4"/ft for 3" and larger, 1/4"/ft for smaller per code).

### Important but Potentially Deferrable

These items strengthen the submission and may be required depending on the
jurisdiction:

8. **Add a proper fixture schedule** — Expand the Water Services table to
   include pipe sizes, fixture unit values, trap sizes, and manufacturer/model
   references. The data model in `app/plumbing.py:FIXTURE_DEFS` already has
   the structure to support additional properties.

9. **Add material annotations** — Label pipe materials (e.g., "3/4\" PEX",
   "3\" PVC SCH 40") on representative runs.

10. **Add water heater details** — Show T&P relief valve with discharge pipe
    (to exterior or floor drain, within 6" of floor), expansion tank, and
    seismic strapping if required by local code.

11. **Add black-and-white dash patterns** — The current color-only
    differentiation fails when printed in monochrome. Add dash patterns that
    distinguish pipe types without relying on color.

### Nice to Have

12. **Show fixture clearances** — Dimension lines from toilets/sinks to nearest
    walls (15" centerline to wall for toilets, etc.).

13. **Add a riser diagram** — Even for a single-story building, a schematic
    riser showing slab penetrations and stub-up heights is helpful.

14. **Add pipe run lengths** — For material takeoff and inspection purposes.

15. **Show insulation** — If hot water pipes require insulation per energy code,
    annotate which runs are insulated.


## Summary

The current plumbing.svg is a well-executed **plan view showing pipe routing
geometry** with accurate spatial relationships to the building structure. The
supply line routing (cold main, hot distribution, water heater T-stub) and the
utility trench are clearly depicted with correct color coding.

However, it functions more as an internal design reference than a permit-ready
plumbing plan. The primary gaps are the absence of a vent system, pipe sizing,
individual drain connections, an isometric drawing, and standard plumbing
symbols. Adding items 1-7 from the recommendations above would bring the plan to
a level suitable for most residential building department submittals.
